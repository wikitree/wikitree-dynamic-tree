Hi WikiTreers,

The Ahnentafel app lists your ancestors well, and the Ahnentafel report feature in the WikiTree Browser Extension makes a report of them, but I wanted one that I could print or save as a PDF without cleaning up first. So I built a WikiTree App called Genealogy Report, and it is ready for testers.

Testers wanted, especially if your tree has adoptions, uncertain parent links, private profiles or several marriages. Those are the places where the report has to make choices, and I most need to know whether it chooses well.

What it does

  • One report for up to 10 generations, numbered the Ahnentafel way (a father is double his child's number, a mother is double plus one). A person who appears twice gets one full entry and "see #n" after that.
  • A full entry for each ancestor: birth and death with places, the path from you, and a Family block with other spouses, siblings and children. Living and private relatives are counted, not named.
  • A cleaned-up biography, without the edit links, stickers and toggles from the profile page. The footnotes become numbered endnotes under each person.
  • WikiTree's own status badges: Research Status (Unfinished to Gold Standard) and Relationship Status (Non-biological, Uncertain, Confident, Confirmed with DNA), in words so they still read when printed in black and white.
  • Biological or adoptive parents, chosen person by person where a profile lists different ones.
  • Optional sections: Statistics, a Fan Chart, a Family Calendar and a Surnames List for exactly the generations in your report, plus indexes of names and locations and a list of the Ahnentafel positions that have no profile yet.
  • Print or Save as PDF. Only the report is printed (not the page header, the form or the AI panel), on a white background, with a new page for each generation and each section, and the fan chart on a landscape page. I have tested saving it as a PDF and it works. Living people can be hidden first, so a copy is safe to share.
  • An optional AI overview, off unless you tick it (see below).

The AI overview, in case it makes you nervous

Ticking AI overview adds a panel beside the finished report. It is never part of the printout, and it only writes a short summary of the tree: where the family came from, how long people lived, the gaps and what to research next. There are two ways to use it:

  • Copy prompt needs no account and no key and sends nothing anywhere. It copies a ready-made prompt that you paste into whichever AI chat you already use, and you can read the prompt first.
  • Generate overview sends the same prompt from your browser to Anthropic Claude, OpenAI or Google Gemini, using your own API key.

You choose each time whether to send facts only, or facts plus a short excerpt of each biography, and the panel says how many ancestors will be sent before you press anything. Living and presumed-living people are never sent. The key is held in the page's memory only: it is never saved in your browser or in the report, and it is forgotten when you leave the view. The result is labelled as AI-written and not checked against the sources. It can be wrong, so treat it as a list of questions to look into. I explain more, and show an example prompt and answer, on the Space page.

What I would love testers to try

  1. Generate a report for your own profile or an ancestor, at 4 to 6 generations. Does it read well on screen? Click Print / Save as PDF and save a PDF: do the page breaks, the fan chart page and the badges look right in your browser and PDF viewer? Browsers differ, so tell me which one you used.
  2. If your tree has an adoption: use the Biological | Adoptive buttons under the ancestor's heading and see whether the report follows the line you expected.
  3. Check the status badges and the "uncertain" or "DNA" wording against what WikiTree shows for the same links.
  4. Look for a relative shown as "private" or "not retrieved" who should be named. Those two words mean different things, and I would like to know if one is wrong.
  5. Try a few sections on and off (Statistics, Fan chart, Family calendar, Surnames list).
  6. If you are willing, try the AI overview. Start with Copy prompt, since it needs no key. Tell me whether the panel made it clear what was being sent.

Good to know

  • It reads the WikiTree API as you, so you see what your login can see. It only works while the app is open on apps.wikitree.com.
  • Only direct-line ancestors get their own entries. Siblings, other spouses and children appear in each ancestor's Family block.
  • The sections are worked out inside the report and do not run the other Tree Apps, so a number can occasionally differ from the app's own. Each section links to the real app.
  • It is a beta and I have only used it in a desktop browser, so it has not been tested on phones.

Feedback

Please answer here, comment on the Space page, or send me a private message. I would especially like to hear what was confusing, what broke, what was slow with a big tree, which biographies came out with leftovers, and whether the AI overview was worth having. If something looks wrong, please include the WikiTree ID of the profile you started from.

Thank you to everyone who built the Tree Apps this borrows from, and to everyone who has put sources on a profile. I wrote this app with the help of Claude, an AI assistant from Anthropic.
